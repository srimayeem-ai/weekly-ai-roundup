Model release10 Nov 2026

Google says Gemini 4 Carbon is now available, and cuts video pricing

Google announced that Gemini 4 Carbon is available, and it cut the price of its Omni 1.2 Flash video model to US$0.07 per second. Earlier coverage named the model "Argon", so check Google's own announcement for the exact product name.

Why it matters: Frontier model access and video generation costs are both falling, which makes both easier to build on.

Leadership12 Nov 2026

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigns; CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO

Nvidia said Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while the board considers internal and external candidates. Nvidia shares fell after the news. Press reports link the departure to personal conduct; Huang has not been charged with anything.

Why it matters: Leadership is changing at a central supplier of AI chips, during a period of heavy AI investment.

Deloitte report2026 · date not confirmed

State of AI in the Enterprise 2026: access is spreading faster than use

Deloitte's survey of more than 3,200 business and IT leaders in 24 countries finds that employee access to enterprise-approved AI tools rose from under 40% to nearly 60% in a year. Fewer than 60% of those with access use it daily. Only 25% of organizations have moved at least 40% of their AI experiments into production, and 20% report revenue growth from AI.

Why it matters: Most enterprises have the tools but have not yet turned them into daily habits, production systems or measurable revenue.

Source: Deloitte
Harvard Business ReviewNov 2024 · earlier piece

How to Be Systematic About Adopting AI at Your Company

John Winsor argues that few organizations have a structured plan for scaling AI. He recommends starting with small, controlled experiments across different parts of the business, then building the ones that work into everyday workflows and supporting infrastructure.

Why it matters: Many enterprises are still stuck at the assessment stage. A staged rollout gives a clearer path from pilots to production.

Source: Harvard Business Review
The EconomistReprint · date unconfirmed

Big tech is spending about $400bn on AI. Will business demand keep up?

An Economist analysis, republished by The Nightly, asks whether the roughly $400bn that big tech is investing in AI will translate into broad business uptake. It considers whether slower adoption, narrower usage or stalled spending could burst the bubble.

Why it matters: Enterprise demand, not just supply of models and data centres, will decide whether this spending pays off.

Source: The Nightly (reprint of The Economist)

UK healthDate unconfirmed

UK accepts all 44 recommendations on regulating AI in healthcare

The UK government has accepted all 44 recommendations of the independent National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare. The recommendations support proportionate oversight across a product's whole lifecycle.

Why it matters: Healthcare AI products will face lifecycle oversight rather than one-off approval.

Source: TLT AI Brief
